# NIVORA pitch: speaker notes

This is the companion script for `NIVORA_Pitch_Deck.pptx`. The same notes are in the PowerPoint presenter view.

Target length: about 6.5 minutes for the 10 slides, not counting a live demo. The timings are only suggestions. No official competition time limit has been confirmed, so adjust them once the rules are known.

---

## Slide 1: The opening: NIVORA

**Time:** 0:20

**Main point:** Introduce NIVORA and the one idea behind it: finding a place to live should feel connected, convenient and trustworthy.

**Script:**

> Good morning, everyone. This is NIVORA, the rental living ecosystem.
>
> Our idea is simple. Finding a place to live should feel connected, convenient and trustworthy. Right now it often doesn't.
>
> In the next few minutes we'll show you the problem, the people we're starting with, what we've already built, and how we plan to turn it into a real service.

**Transition:** Let's start with what renting actually feels like today.

**Caveat to keep in mind:** Introduce the team only by details the team has confirmed. Don't use a team name, competition name or affiliation that hasn't been confirmed. The cover photo is an illustrative stock image (Pexels), not a NIVORA property.

---

## Slide 2: The problem: renting is fragmented

**Time:** 0:45

**Main point:** The problem isn't only finding a property. It's a chain of related decisions with no connected experience.

**Script:**

> Picture a student arriving in a new city for college, or someone starting a new job.
>
> Finding a home isn't one task. They search across portals, brokers, groups and word of mouth. They compare places whose details don't line up. They try to work out whether a listing is genuine. They arrange calls and visits on separate channels. Then come the agreement, the deposit and the move.
>
> Every step happens somewhere else.
>
> This is not a niche situation. The 2011 Census counted about 2.17 crore urban households living in rented homes. And NSSO data, cited in a 2019 Knight Frank and Khaitan & Co. report, found that 71 percent of renting households had no written agreement. Much of renting in India is still informal.
>
> So the problem isn't just finding a property. It's getting through a whole set of decisions without one connected experience.

**Transition:** So who feels this most, and where do we start?

**Caveat to keep in mind:** The five stages describe the problem as we frame it. They are not survey findings. Both figures are national and dated (Census 2011, and NSSO data quoted in a 2019 report), so don't present them as Jaipur figures or current numbers. The 71 percent figure is about written agreements, not scams or fraud. Don't add statistics about scams, broker fees or time wasted.

## Slide 3: The user: who we are solving this for

**Time:** 0:40

**Main point:** We start with a focused audience whose search is tied to a specific place: students and working professionals.

**Script:**

> We're not building for everyone on day one.
>
> We start with two groups. Students, who need a place near their college or university and have to balance distance, budget, room type and everyday convenience. And working professionals, who want a home within practical reach of their workplace.
>
> Both share the same core needs: the right location, the right budget, living preferences that suit them, and confidence that the listing is real.
>
> Property owners are the other side of the platform. Every renter's journey depends on them, and we'll come to them in the business model.
>
> Our proposed starting point is focused rental discovery around selected college and office clusters in Jaipur.

**Transition:** Here's how NIVORA brings that journey together.

**Caveat to keep in mind:** Jaipur is a proposed launch strategy, not proven demand. We have no user research, survey or interview results yet, so don't quote any. The photos are illustrative stock interiors, not real users or NIVORA listings. Expanding to other segments depends on validating this first use case.

## Slide 4: The solution: introducing NIVORA

**Time:** 0:40

**Main point:** NIVORA is designed as one connected experience, not just another directory of listings.

**Script:**

> NIVORA brings the important parts of the rental journey into one experience. We think about it in three parts.
>
> Discover: you search around a place that matters to you, such as a college or a workplace, within a radius you choose, and filter by budget and preferences.
>
> Make informed choices: each listing shows its details and four separate trust checks, so you can see what has been checked and what hasn't.
>
> Connect the journey: the tenant and owner views already share the same visit requests. Agreements, payments and move-in support will come only as we actually build them.
>
> This is the welcome screen of our working prototype.

**Transition:** Rather than describe it, let me show you the experience.

**Caveat to keep in mind:** Be clear about what exists now and what doesn't. Discovery and trust labels run on fictional demo data. No real verification, payments, agreements or production backend exist. The screenshot is the real prototype interface.

## Slide 5: The product: from search to property discovery

**Time:** 0:50

**Main point:** The working prototype makes the experience tangible: a guided, location-led search from first question to matching homes.

**Script:**

> This is the prototype as it runs today.
>
> First, you meet Nivi, our guide. She asks one question at a time, starting with your name.
>
> Next, you choose where to search around. In this demo it's Vivekananda Global University, which we use as a test destination. Then you set a radius from 2 to 10 kilometres. It's straight-line distance on the map, and we say so on screen.
>
> Then you share preferences: furnishing, budget, home type, bedrooms and must-haves.
>
> Finally, you see the matching homes. Each card shows the distance from your destination and how many checks it has, and you can open any home to see its details and request a visit.
>
> Every listing here is demonstration data, and we label it that way inside the product.

**Transition:** That brings us to what we think makes NIVORA different, and to trust.

**Caveat to keep in mind:** Listings, prices and owners are fictional. Map positions are simulated. Photos are illustrative stock images (Pexels), not the actual homes. Distances are straight-line, not travel time. VGU is a test destination at an approximate location, with no affiliation or endorsement. Don't call these results live availability. The radius and place screenshots come from the project's evidence folder (10 Oct 2026). The results screenshot was captured from the demo build on 12 Oct 2026 and cropped to the listing cards, because map tiles couldn't load in that environment.

## Slide 6: Trust and differentiation

**Time:** 0:45

**Main point:** Our difference is a strategy: location-relevant discovery, trust that is itemised and honest, and a journey that stays connected.

**Script:**

> Where's the difference?
>
> First, discovery starts from a place that matters to you, such as your college or office, not just a city name.
>
> Second, trust is built in. A single 'verified' tick can hide a lot, so we split trust into four checks: who the owner is, whether they can rent the home, whether the listing is accurate, and whether it's still available. Each one shows its status separately.
>
> To be clear, in the prototype these are demo labels. A real system would need defined checks, supporting evidence, transparency for renters, and someone accountable for the process. That is part of our roadmap.
>
> Third, discovery, visit requests and owner follow-up already sit in one shared flow, and we'll extend it only as each stage becomes real.

**Transition:** So how does this become a business?

**Caveat to keep in mind:** We make no claims about what competitors lack. Present this as our strategy, not as superiority. Verification is simulated: nothing about a real person or property has been checked. Location search has one founder-designated test destination, and the wider location verification work isn't complete.

## Slide 7: The business model: how NIVORA can earn

**Time:** 0:40

**Main point:** A proposed two-sided model with two possible revenue streams. It's a hypothesis to validate, not current revenue.

**Script:**

> NIVORA sits between two groups. Tenants get relevant homes, clearer information and a guided journey. Owners get suitable enquiries and an organised way to manage listings.
>
> We see two possible revenue streams.
>
> One is a clearly disclosed fee linked to a completed rental, but only if it turns out to be legally appropriate and workable in practice.
>
> The other is optional paid tools for owners, such as listing management or rent and maintenance tracking, but only where owners see genuine value.
>
> We haven't set prices. Before we do, we need evidence on willingness to pay, unit economics, the cost of acquiring users, and the real cost of delivering verification.

**Transition:** Which brings us to where we start, and how we earn the right to grow.

**Caveat to keep in mind:** No pricing, take rate, revenue, projections or customer numbers exist, so don't give any. Rent and maintenance tracking exist in the prototype only as local demo records. Any fee model must be checked against applicable law and regulations.

## Slide 8: Market opportunity and go-to-market

**Time:** 0:40

**Main point:** Start narrow in Jaipur, prove it works, then expand only with evidence.

**Script:**

> The national picture shows renting and student mobility are significant. About 2.17 crore urban households rented their homes in 2011, and India had about 4.33 crore students in higher education in 2021-22.
>
> But that is not our addressable market, and we won't pretend it is.
>
> Our plan is to start small. Phase one is a focused Jaipur pilot: a few college or office clusters, real owners, and genuine listings. Phase two is validation: feedback from tenants and owners, and measuring things like listing relevance and completed enquiries. Phase three is controlled expansion, only once we have evidence of demand, listing quality and sustainable growth.
>
> Sizing the Jaipur opportunity is itself part of the pilot. We'll do it cluster by cluster.

**Transition:** Here's what it takes to make that plan real.

**Caveat to keep in mind:** Both figures are national, from different years and different measures. Don't add them together or treat them as a TAM. No Jaipur market size has been estimated yet. No pilot has happened and there are no results.

## Slide 9: Execution, feasibility and roadmap

**Time:** 0:45

**Main point:** Be honest about what exists, what comes next, and how we reduce risk.

**Script:**

> Here's where we honestly are.
>
> We have a working prototype: the brand intro, guided onboarding with Nivi, radius search around a test destination, demo listings with itemised checks, and tenant and owner views that share the same visit requests. Its automated test suite passes.
>
> What we don't have yet is the real-world operation, and the next three stages are about building it.
>
> Stage one: validate. We'll refine the tenant experience, talk to renters and owners, and agree in advance what pilot success looks like.
>
> Stage two: build the foundation. That means real verification procedures, a production backend, proper privacy and data handling, clear support responsibilities, and legal checks before we touch agreements or payments.
>
> Stage three: a controlled pilot with genuine listings. We'll measure outcomes and improve before expanding.
>
> These are the measures we'll track. We haven't set targets, because we don't yet have a baseline.

**Transition:** Let me close where we started.

**Caveat to keep in mind:** All three stages are proposed. Don't give dates, funding, partners or team capabilities that haven't been confirmed. The release-candidate report (11 Oct 2026) records 520 passing automated tests. It also says the prototype is not yet signed off as demo-ready until the founder's checks and a physical rehearsal are done. No pilot metrics have been measured.
